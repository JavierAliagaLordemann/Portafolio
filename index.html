 ABOUT

Who I am?

I am Javier Aliaga Lordemann, economic-financial analyst, and modeler specializing in transition economies, natural resources, commodities, and investment analysis.

Throughout my career, I have worked in more than 40 countries across Latin America, Africa, Europe, and Asia, supporting governments, international cooperation agencies, development banks, non-governmental organizations, and financial institutions in generating evidence-based analysis for macroeconomic, sectoral, and investment decision-making.

For more than 20 years, my work has focused on the intersection of macroeconomics, finance, investment analysis, energy, climate change, natural resources, and environmental sustainability. My expertise covers energy transition, energy efficiency, renewable energy integration, physical and transition climate risk assessment, climate-smart agriculture, biodiversity, commodity markets, and strategic investment evaluation.

I combine techno-economic, financial, econometric, and stochastic modelling with scenario analysis, stress testing, project valuation, and bankability assessment. I develop quantitative models to evaluate costs, returns, cash flows, fiscal impacts, and exposure to commodity prices, exchange rates, interest rates, and regulatory change. I also use data analytics, Python, and AI and machine-learning tools to strengthen forecasting, risk assessment, and investment decision-making.

My work is dedicated to transforming complex economic, financial, environmental, and sectoral information into practical recommendations for public policy, investment, and financing decisions.


Areas of Expertise

I provide techno-economic, financial, regulatory, and quantitative analysis to support energy-transition policies, investments, and strategic decisions.

  • Energy investment and regulatory analysis: Assessment of energy mixes, renewable integration, energy efficiency, storage, electrification, distributed generation, industrial self-generation, and fossil-fuel substitution. This includes project valuation, LCOE and LACE analysis, CAPEX and OPEX modeling, WACC, NPV, IRR, DSCR, sensitivity and stress testing, tariff design, auctions, PPAs, subsidies, taxation, regulatory risk, bankability, and public-private investment structures.
  • Energy-economy and transition modeling: Development of energy, macro-energy, econometric, financial, stochastic, and scenario models to assess decarbonization pathways, energy demand, productive transformation, macroeconomic impacts, fiscal sustainability, mitigation, and adaptation. Approaches include CGE-3E, input-output, macro-sectoral, integrated energy-economy, climate-economy, and long-term planning models.
  • Power-system planning and market analysis: Analysis of generation and transmission expansion, economic dispatch, marginal pricing, electricity markets, capacity adequacy, reliability, reserve margins, congestion, losses, renewable penetration, flexibility, storage, residual load, and climate-related variability in electricity supply and demand.
  • Energy, commodity, and hydrocarbon markets: Analysis of oil, natural gas, electricity, biofuels, hydrogen, critical minerals, fertilizers, metals, food, and carbon markets. The work covers prices, volatility, contracts, inventories, supply and demand shocks, geopolitical and logistics risks, exchange rates, interest rates, energy security, and their effects on investment, competitiveness, inflation, and external accounts.
  • Hydrocarbon-sector economics: Field economic evaluation, refinery optimization, fuel blending, structural reform, subsidy reform, and analysis of the fiscal, inflationary, balance-of-payments, employment, investment, and sectoral effects of energy-price changes.
  • Climate and transition-risk assessment: Measurement of greenhouse-gas emissions, carbon intensity, abatement costs, MACC curves, physical and transition risks, local pollution, energy efficiency, and the environmental, social, and economic co-benefits of aligning energy policy with climate and biodiversity objectives.

Selected tools: Excel, Python, R, Stata, EViews, GAMS, LEAP, TIMES, MESSAGE, OSeMOSYS, PLEXOS, POLES, OPTGEN, HOMER, RETScreen, Crystal Ball, and @RISK.

I provide quantitative, economic, financial, and strategic analysis to assess climate risks, design mitigation and adaptation strategies, and integrate climate considerations into policies, investments, sectoral planning, and climate-finance decisions.

  • Climate strategy and scenario analysis: Design of mitigation, adaptation, and resilience strategies supported by forward-looking scenarios under uncertainty. The analysis incorporates emissions pathways, climate policies, carbon pricing, technological change, regulatory constraints, sectoral exposure, financing needs, and alternative transition trajectories.
  • Physical climate-risk assessment: Evaluation of risks arising from droughts, floods, heat waves, water stress, precipitation variability, agricultural losses, infrastructure damage, logistical disruptions, ecosystem degradation, and territorial vulnerability. The analysis combines hazard, exposure, sensitivity, and adaptive capacity with economic estimates of losses, adaptation costs, asset impacts, productivity effects, and resilience benefits.
  • Transition-risk assessment: Analysis of regulatory, technological, market, and financial risks associated with decarbonization, including stranded assets, carbon pricing, fossil-fuel subsidy reform, electrification, technological substitution, changing demand for fuels and commodities, credit risk, access to finance, sectoral competitiveness, public finance, and project bankability.

Climate-economic and financial modeling: Development of climate-economic, techno-economic, financial, econometric, and stochastic models to estimate impacts on growth, inflation, employment, productivity, investment, fiscal accounts, balance of payments, cash flows, cost of capital, and financial sustainability. Methods include climate stress testing, sensitivity analysis, risk matrices, damage functions, adaptation-cost assessment, abatement-cost analysis, and resilience pathways. Nature, agriculture, and natural-resource resilience: Assessment of biodiversity, ecosystems, climate-smart agriculture, food security, bioeconomy, agricultural insurance, adaptation finance, water management, nature-based solutions, landscape restoration, and sustainable rural investment. This includes the economic and financial evaluation of agricultural technologies, productive practices, and resilience investments. Selected tools: AquaCrop, DSSAT, CropSyst, SCALE UP, geospatial analysis, climate scenarios, financial models, stochastic simulation, and integrated biophysical, economic, and climate-risk assessment tools..


How I Work

My work is based on four fundamental principles that ensure solid, practical, and actionable results:

Evidence Over Opinion

Recommendations are based on economic, financial, sectoral, data-driven, quantitative models, and systemic analysis, fully documented and reproducible.

Rigor with Practical Relevance

Technical analysis is only valuable if decision-makers can use it effectively and translate it into viable and applicable solutions.

Interdisciplinarity

Energy, climate, environmental, and agricultural challenges require the coherent integration of economics, finance, science, technology, institutions, and governance.

Clear Communication

I translate complex results into understandable and practical messages for policymakers, technical teams, investors, and key stakeholders.

I work flexibly, adapting to the needs of each project or consultancy. I offer both remote and on-site support, as well as direct collaboration with teams requiring strategic advisory services. In addition, I contribute to strengthening the technical and strategic capacities of the teams with which I work.
